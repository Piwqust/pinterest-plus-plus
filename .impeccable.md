## Design Context

### Users
Pinterest users who collect visual references and need a faster way to reuse original-quality media or inspect dense visual grids. The primary jobs are copying an image, copying a video's first frame, downloading the original media, gathering many pins into folders and saving them in one go, and enlarging grid tiles without leaving the page.

### Brand Personality
Native, quiet, precise. Interactions should feel confidently integrated with Pinterest while remaining clearly extension-owned.

### Aesthetic Direction
A restrained light interface using Pinterest-adjacent proportions, warm white surfaces, near-black text, and red only for active emphasis. Controls are compact, tactile, and progressively disclosed. Avoid generic dashboards, gradients, glass effects, decorative cards, and visually loud overlays.

The extension's own surfaces (the toolbar popup, the store listing) wear the brand of the mark instead: a graphite keycap lit in white. Near-black canvas, faint white bloom and grid, Inter, white as the only colour; no brand hue and nothing near Pinterest red. The on-page controls stay light because they sit on Pinterest's page. The collection panel is the exception that proves the rule: it is the extension's own workspace laid over the page rather than a control on a pin, so it wears the dark brand like the popup. Its launcher is not: it is a light square key beside the grid slider, as tall as it, so the pair reads as one control; only its graphite count badge points at the dark panel it opens.

### Design Principles
- Keep media first: controls appear on intent and never permanently cover the image.
- Match Pinterest's visual rhythm without impersonating first-party controls.
- Make one-click actions immediate, legible, and reversible where possible.
- Preserve accessibility with keyboard focus, 44px targets, reduced motion, and text feedback.
- Prefer resilient DOM heuristics and isolated styles over fragile class-name selectors.
